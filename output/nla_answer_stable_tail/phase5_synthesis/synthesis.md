# Cross-paper synthesis

The evidence supports a modest behavioral premise: in several model/task settings, correct or mutually consistent answers arise before generation ends, and stopping policies can save tokens with little average loss. It also supports a representational premise: hidden states can predict answer correctness. Neither premise identifies a distinct internal self-verification process, nor demonstrates that a stable suffix is redundant on an individual trace.

NLA's incremental value is **open-ended semantic hypothesis generation**. Its cost is weak claim-level calibration. Therefore it belongs after independently selected candidate boundaries and alongside probes/geometry, before causal experiments—not as the detector or final validator.
