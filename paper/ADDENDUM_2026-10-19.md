# Addendum to the frozen report (2026-10-19)

The report is frozen at `32b5539` (D66), and its sections are not edited. This
addendum records corrections to how some of its results should be read. They
come from a post-hoc audit made after the freeze (D68). **No registered number
changes.** What changes is what some of the numbers can be taken to mean. The
evidence is in [`docs/SCIENTIFIC_AUDIT_2026-10-19.md`](../docs/SCIENTIFIC_AUDIT_2026-10-19.md),
and every figure below is regenerated by
`scripts/audit_claims_posthoc.py --config main`. Every analysis cited here is
**post hoc and exploratory**.

## A1. The primary comparison cannot speak to incremental value (§6.3, §7.1, §7.3)

The registered result stands as reported: no significant difference between
the readout and semantic entropy (q = 0.115) or the probe (q = 1.0). Three
facts, all measured from the stored artifacts, limit what it means.

- **The comparison was made where no rule can be mostly safe.** A rule that
  stops only where stopping is safe can save at most a mean 0.174 of tokens
  under the outcome as implemented. The head-to-heads were made at 0.613 and
  0.722.
- **The safe rates there are mostly abstention.** A rule that never fires on
  a problem counts as safe on it. 38 of semantic entropy's 39 safe problems
  are problems it never stopped on; when it stopped it was safe on 1 of 82.
- **A rule using no signal beats every compared rule.** A rule that stops at
  the first sentence on a random subset of problems scores 0.373 [0.350,
  0.392] at 0.613, against 0.325 for semantic entropy and 0.233 for the
  readout. At 0.722 it scores 0.261 [0.242, 0.275], against 0.133 for the
  probe and 0.125 for the readout.

**Corrected reading.** The O3 result is not evidence about whether the
verbalised readout adds value beyond cheaper signals, in either direction.
§6.3's "directionally worse than semantic entropy", §7.1's remark that the
N = 240 direction is "more credible", and §7.3's account of semantic
entropy's advantage should not be read as information content. That advantage
comes from always stopping at the first boundary, which reaches a budget with
the most abstentions. The abstract's last sentence ("did not demonstrate
value") remains literally true. The design could not have demonstrated it.

## A2. The implemented safety outcome is not the registered one (§3.5)

§3.5 and PROJECT_PLAN §5 define safety on the answer **forced** from the
prefix. The code scores the answer **parsed** from the prefix text
(`scripts/score_baselines.py`, `build_rows`). This was not recorded as a
deviation before the freeze. Stopping at the tail start is safe on 0.275 of
the NLA problem set under the implemented outcome, and on 1.000 under the
registered one. No O3 number has been recomputed under the registered outcome.
The stored artifacts lack forced answers at most early boundaries.

## A3. "Matched budget" is matched trace tokens, not matched compute (§1, §5.1)

The plan's O3 asks for "matched compute". The implementation matches the share
of the trace not generated. Computing semantic entropy costs 8.2× the trace's
own tokens, and the verbaliser 9.6×. Neither cost is counted.

## A4. The tail marks recoverability, not determinacy (§3.3, §6.2, §7.1)

Where the tail begins before the trace writes its answer, the 48-token forced
completion does the remaining arithmetic itself on 0.595 of problems. The tail
start marks where the answer can be **reliably completed within the forcing
budget**. That is narrower than "the answer is determined". Its sensitivity to
that budget is already in §4.3. Only a mean 0.117 of a trace's tokens follow
the first written answer. The tail is mostly answer-producing computation, not
redundant text.

## A5. RQ1's association is not separable from trace length (§6.2)

The registered test reproduces. With log trace length and dataset as
covariates, tail fraction no longer predicts correctness (coefficient 0.86,
p = 0.68; 21 incorrect answers). Whether tail fraction carries information
beyond length is untested.

## A6. F10 and RQ2 use non-independent units; the family count changes (§5.5, §6.4, §6.7)

The registered F10 tests compare about four samples per problem, and RQ2's
test compares 6,007 claims from 120 problems, both as if the units were
independent. At the problem level:

- RQ2 is unchanged: mean 0.0324 [0.0301, 0.0347], positive on all 120
  problems.
- F10's tail-versus-matched-length contrast has p = 0.053, and the mean
  tail-versus-matched-position difference is −0.0052 [−0.0119, 0.0016].
- Substituting these into the BH family gives **4** significant tests under
  the evaluable convention and **3** under the planned family of nine, not 5
  under both. RQ1 is significant under one convention and not the other.

So §5.5/§6.7's "the convention decides nothing" holds only under the
registered units. F10's reading that fidelity is not higher in the tail is
unaffected.

## A7. RQ2 establishes reconstruction dependence and nothing about content (§6.4, §7.1)

The audit confirms the registered reading and adds two limits. 29% of the
paraphrase-null draws leave the claim text unchanged, so the null is
narrower than "meaning-preserving rewording". And no validated test of claim
content exists (amendment A1 did not corroborate). Semantic accuracy,
faithfulness to the computation and mechanism all remain untested.

## A8. RQ3: the diagnosis, extended (§6.5, §8.1, §8.3)

RQ3 stays not tested. Three findings extend the diagnosis:

- **The construct is near-absent.** A broader verification lexicon finds 12
  of 250 traces.
- **The random-direction control is not matched on edit size.** The candidate
  removes about 19× more of each activation (21.8% against 1.1% of its norm).
- **Answer change rose only at doses with no controls.** At those doses the
  output roughly tripled in length.

The decision record's description of the controls as "unchanged" (D62, RQ3
brief) is not a contrast, because they ran only at dose 1.0, where the
candidate changed 1 of 60 answers. §6.5 already says so correctly. A
follow-up design is in `docs/RQ3_FOLLOWUP_DESIGN.md`. It is a proposal and
has not been run.

*Later the same day (D74):* every one of the 13 answer changes at dose 2.0 is
a continuation cut off by the 200-token cap, and so are 3 of the 4 at dose
1.5. The exploratory answer-change dose–response in §6.5 is therefore not
evidence that the edit changes answers.

## A9. Corrected reanalyses (D69–D73)

These are corrected or post hoc. Details are in
[`docs/REANALYSIS_2026-10-19.md`](../docs/REANALYSIS_2026-10-19.md).

**O3 under the registered outcome (D69).** Forced answers were completed at
all 1,236 boundaries that lacked one. The instrument reproduced on 194 of 200
re-forced stored boundaries. The plan was hashed before any number existed.
Every rule was given the same positions to stop at, because the readout's
windows straddle the tail start and would otherwise leak the outcome.
Budgets lay below this outcome's oracle bound (0.362).

On the 120 registered problems:
- **The readout's score beats a no-signal rule:** +0.052 [0.022, 0.082]. The
  probe and convergence do not, significantly. This is the first evidence in
  the study that the readout carries stopping-relevant information.
- **Against the probe it is unresolved:** +0.027 [−0.026, 0.076].
- **Against semantic entropy it cannot be compared.** 82 of 120 problems tie
  at confidence 1.0, so it has no operating point below the oracle bound.
- **The pre-specified overall verdict is *unresolved*.**

On the post-hoc 240-problem arm the readout also beats the probe: +0.057
[0.023, 0.091]. That does not hold against a layer-24 probe or in the
all-boundaries design (D73).

**RQ2's null (D70).** Removing the identity and normalised-identity draws
gives dependence rates of 0.730 (all valid draws) and 0.775 (surface-verified
draws), against 0.750 registered. The conclusion holds. The null remains
a surface-edit null, not a test of rewording.

**What the claims say (D71).** Checked objectively against the trace:
- The stated next word is right on 52% of explanations, against 3–10% under
  the controls.
- The quoted final token's word is right on only 2%, though its last
  character is right on 73%.
- Quotations of context are essentially never verbatim (0.3%).
- Interpretive and mechanistic content remains unvalidated. A human protocol
  exists; it has not been run.

**F10 and RQ2 at the problem level (D72).** RQ2 is unchanged. F10's tail
versus matched-window differences are not robust. With the corrected units,
the BH family has 2–4 significant tests, not 5, and **RQ1 is not robustly
significant**.
